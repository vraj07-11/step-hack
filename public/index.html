Frequently Asked Questions

How does StepHack log steps into Google Fit?
StepHack connects directly to the official Google Fitness REST API using OAuth 2.0. When you input your step count and walk duration, it generates realistic 30-second pedometer intervals with human-like cadence variation, calculating synchronized walking distance (meters) and calories burned before updating your Google Fit timeline.
Why does StepHack distribute steps across time instead of adding a lump sum?
Standard manual activity additions in fitness apps create a single artificial spike that third-party sync engines and health programs often ignore. By distributing steps across realistic 30-second cadence chunks with warm-up and cool-down momentum, Google Fit registers the session as genuine continuous movement.
Do logged steps sync with Pokémon GO Adventure Sync and Health Connect?
Yes. Because StepHack registers data points under phone hardware sensor streams (com.google.step_count.delta on device type phone), Google Fit aggregates the steps into your daily totals, allowing apps that read from Google Fit or Health Connect to recognize the activity.
Is StepHack safe and private to use?

Yes, 100%. StepHack is fully open-source and operates strictly in compliance with Google API Services User Data Policies. You can audit and verify the entire application source code directly in the public GitHub repository:

github.com/vraj07-11/step-hack

Your authentication tokens are stored exclusively in your own browser's encrypted session cookies. StepHack has no external database, collects no telemetry, and never stores, logs, or shares your personal fitness records with third parties.

⚠️ How to bypass the “Google hasn't verified this app” warning:

Because StepHack is a free, non-commercial open-source personal project, Google's OAuth consent screen displays an “unverified app” warning. This is standard for independent developer applications. To log in safely:

  1. On the warning screen, click the small “Advanced” link in the bottom-left corner.
  2. Click “Go to StepHack (unsafe)” to continue to the authorization screen.
  3. Check the permissions to allow StepHack to record walking data into Google Fit, then tap “Continue”.
What is the recommended walking cadence for realistic tracking?
Natural human walking pace typically averages between 100 and 115 steps per minute (~4.5 to 5.0 km/h). Use the “Auto-fit pace” feature in the duration slider to automatically calibrate the optimal duration for your step count so Google Fit doesn't flag high-cadence anomalies.
Do I need to physically own the phone selected in the device profile?
No. You can use StepHack from any device (phone, tablet, or desktop). The device profile provides virtual hardware sensor metadata to Google Fit's data collector so your session registers as genuine phone pedometer hardware readings rather than an untracked manual entry. We recommend Google Pixel 8 Pro as it offers native first-party sensor stream compatibility with Google Fit.
Do I have to keep the website open or running in the background?
No, not at all. Once you tap Sync Steps to Google Fit and receive the confirmation toast, your workout data is immediately written to Google's cloud servers. You can safely close the tab, exit your browser, or lock your phone right away—your steps are already permanently saved in your Google Fit timeline.
